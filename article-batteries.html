The Truth About Unbranded Amazon PS5 Batteries: Fake Ratings & Hidden Safety Risks

By 512 Controller Clinic | Austin, TX • Battery Safety & Bench Testing

If you have ever searched Amazon for a replacement PS5 DualSense battery, you have probably seen dozens of unbranded listings promising astronomical numbers—4000mAh, 4800mAh, or even higher—for $12 to $15. On paper, it sounds like triple the battery life of Sony's stock cell for pocket change.

In reality, most of those numbers are physically impossible for the size of a DualSense battery tray. Worse yet, inflated capacity labels aren't the only corners these no-name suppliers are cutting. Inside many of those cheap packs is a hidden safety shortcut that puts your controller—and your hands—at risk.

The Fake mAh Rating Epidemic

A factory Sony DualSense (LIP1708) battery is rated at 1560mAh at 3.65V, which works out to roughly 5700mWh (5.7Wh) of stored energy. Lithium-ion battery chemistry has strict physical limits: you can only pack so much energy density into a fixed plastic shell.

When an unbranded Amazon seller slaps a "4000mAh" or "4800mAh" sticker on a battery the exact same size as Sony's original, it isn't a breakthrough in battery science—it's a lie. Under real discharge testing, many of these cheap packs deliver less than half of what is printed on the label, and some perform even worse than a worn-out stock Sony battery.

The Real Danger: Missing Thermistors & Fake Resistors

A bad capacity rating is annoying, but bypassing thermal protection is genuinely dangerous. Every genuine PS5 DualSense battery has three wires: positive (red), negative (black), and a third wire connected to a built-in thermistor.

What is a Thermistor?
A thermistor (short for thermal resistor) is a tiny temperature-sensitive sensor attached directly to the lithium battery cell. As the battery's temperature rises or drops, the thermistor's electrical resistance changes. Your PS5 controller constantly reads this signal—if the battery starts overheating while charging or during heavy gameplay, the controller's charging circuit immediately throttles or shuts off power to prevent damage.

To save pennies during manufacturing, many cheap aftermarket battery suppliers leave out the real thermistor altogether. Because the PS5 controller will refuse to charge if that third wire is missing, these suppliers solder a cheap, static (fixed) resistor onto the protection board instead.

Why a Static Resistor Is Dangerous:
A static resistor never changes its value—no matter how hot the battery gets. It permanently tricks the DualSense into thinking the battery is sitting at a safe room temperature, even if the cell is actively overheating. Without working thermal cutoff protection, a failing lithium cell can swell inside your controller shell, damage your motherboard, or in worst-case scenarios, vent or catch fire while charging unattended.
